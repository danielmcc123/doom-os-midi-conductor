Manual

Everything the MIDI Conductor adds to DOOM OS, and the buttons that run it.

Experimental. Flashing modified firmware can brick your SP-404MKII, and this has only been tested on one unit running Roland 5.52 with DOOM OS 0.6.2-alpha (build 3824). The patch changes a few of Roland's own routines, which adds risk of its own. Keep the original Roland files so you can go back. Use at your own risk.

Quick start

  1. Choose your synths in the patcher (optional). The CC lists decide which synths the SP knows by name, so a CTRL knob can pick CUTOFF instead of a number. See CC lists.
  2. SHIFT + REMAIN opens MIDI SETTINGS. Open DEVICES and give each synth a channel and a name. Open PADS, tap a pad and give it a device or channel.
  3. SHIFT + FX1..FX6 turns MIDI mode on and picks a bank. The CTRL knobs now send CC.
  4. MARK edits which device, channel and CC each CTRL sends.

Reading the screens

Every MIDI screen has a title bar and a line of help at the bottom. On the help line, a name drawn on a lit box is a button or knob on the SP, and the plain words after it say what it does. For example, VALUE CHANGES BANK means turning the VALUE knob changes the bank. C1, C2 and C3 are the CTRL 1, 2 and 3 knobs.

The MIDI SETTINGS menu

SHIFT + REMAIN opens the menu from any DOOM screen. MIDI mode can be off.

  • Turn VALUE to move the highlight, press VALUE to open the view.
  • Press VALUE or EXIT inside a view to come back to the menu.
  • EXIT in the menu, or SHIFT + REMAIN anywhere, closes it.

Pad channels and devices

Every pad sends its notes on its own channel. A pad with no channel sends no note at all. A device is a named synth with a channel of its own; a pad that follows a device uses that device's channel, so changing the device's channel moves every pad that uses it.

PADS

  • Turn VALUE to pick the pad bank A to J. Tap a pad to select it. The square shows the bank and pad, for example C4.
  • CTRL 1 sets the pad's device or channel: the knob runs through devices 1 to 7, then channels 1 to 16. CTRL 3 clears it.
  • The screen says NOT SET for a pad with nothing, CH 5 for a plain channel, and DEV MINILOG for a device. A device that has no channel yet shows CH NOT SET.

DEVICES

  • CTRL 1 picks the device, 1 to 7 (VALUE turns pick it too).
  • CTRL 2 sets the device's name. The names are your chosen synths first, then DRUMS, BASS, KEYS, SYNTH, LEAD, PAD and FX.
  • CTRL 3 sets its channel, 1 to 16. All the way down is --, no channel.

The pad screen and the MIDI page show just the device name.

CC lists

A device named after one of the supported synths carries that synth's CC list. A CTRL that follows such a device picks its CC by name, and the MIDI page shows the name. Other devices, and CTRLs that don't follow a device, send raw CC numbers, so every synth works.

You choose the lists in the patcher before you build the firmware. Nothing is pre-selected: use + Add a synth and pick from the list. The ✕ on a row removes it. The synths you add take the device name slots on the SP in the order shown, followed by the generic names.

Your own synth

Not on the list? Use the JSON template or CSV template links under the list, fill in your synth's CC numbers and names, and add the file in the same panel. A custom list has a device name of 1 to 7 characters and CC names of 1 to 7 characters (A to Z, 0 to 9, space and - + . / #). There is room for 11 device names and 250 CC names in all. download this list saves the current choice as a starting point.

Sequence your synths

The SP's sequencer can play your external synths. Every pad hit in a pattern goes out of the DIN MIDI output as a note, on that pad's channel. Put different pads on different channels and one pattern plays several synths at once.

  1. Give the pad a channel. SHIFT + REMAIN, open PADS, tap the pad and use CTRL 1 to pick a channel or a device. A pad with no channel sends no note.
  2. Make the pad playable. A pad needs a sample before the sequencer will play it. To play a synth without any sound from the SP, record silence into the pad (sample with nothing coming in, or the input turned down) instead of loading a sample and muting it.
  3. Record or enter the pattern in DOOM OS as usual. Each step sends the pad's note on the pad's channel.
  4. Play melodies from one pad. In the piano roll, each step's pitch is added to the pad's note.
  5. Add movement. Record your CTRL moves into the pattern (see Recording knob moves) and they play back as CC on the same loop.

Notes end at the step's gate, not at the end of the sample, so a short silent sample is fine. STOP and choke send no note-off yet, so a note can be left hanging; an all-notes-off or panic on the synth clears it.

MIDI mode and banks

In MIDI mode the CTRL knobs send MIDI instead of changing the effect. There are 36 CTRLs in six banks of six. The MIDI page shows three CTRLs at a time: the CTRL number, its value and a bar, then the device (or channel) and the CC.

  • SHIFT + FX1..FX6 chooses bank 1 to 6 and turns MIDI mode on. The FX key of the active bank is lit. The title bar says which bank you are on.
  • Press VALUE to flip between CTRL pages 1/2 and 2/2 (CTRL 1 to 3, then 4 to 6). The page number is at the right of the title bar.
  • MARK edits the CTRLs (below).
  • EXIT leaves MIDI mode and returns to DOOM OS.

Recording knob moves

Record your CTRL moves into a pattern and have them play back as CC. Each CTRL has its own lane of 64 points across the pattern, so slow sweeps stay smooth.

  • SHIFT + VALUE turns recording on and off while MIDI mode is on. The page shows REC.
  • While recording, a CTRL you move is written at every point the pattern passes. A CTRL you touch keeps recording its current value until you stop.
  • The recorded moves play back as the pattern runs, with MIDI mode on or off, and stop when the sequencer stops.
  • Turn a CTRL by hand during playback and it takes over from its lane until the pattern loops.
  • SHIFT + EXIT erases every lane and leaves MIDI mode on.

All patterns share the same lanes, and they are kept in RAM only, so they are cleared when you power off.

Editing a CTRL

MARK turns edit mode on and off on the MIDI page. In edit mode, turn VALUE to pick the CTRL; its box moves. Then use the CTRL knobs:

  • CTRL 1 chooses its device or MIDI channel. The knob runs through devices 1 to 7, then channels 1 to 16.
  • CTRL 2 chooses its CC. For a device with a CC list it picks by name.

If a CTRL follows a device, the page shows the device's name. Changing a device's channel updates every pad and CTRL that uses it. All settings are saved in DOOM OS's settings file and survive a power cycle.

Limits

  • One MIDI output (DIN). USB MIDI isn't covered.
  • Knob moves in MIDI mode don't change the SP's own effect.
  • 11 device names and 250 CC names in total; 7 devices to give channels to.
  • If a synth echoes CC back into the SP, feedback is possible. Untested.
  • Only Roland 5.52 with DOOM OS 0.6.2-alpha (3824) is supported. DOOM OS is built into the patch, and the patcher refuses any file other than stock Roland 5.52, so a newer Roland or DOOM OS release can't be patched until it has been tested here.
